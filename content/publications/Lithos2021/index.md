abstract: "Continental crust formation above subduction zones depends heavily on how magma evolves as minerals crystallize deep underground. The mineral amphibole plays a pivotal role in this process, but direct geological evidence of its formation has been scarce. In this study, we conducted petrological and geochemical analyses of amphibole-rich ultramafic rocks from the Hida Metamorphic Complex in central Japan. Our findings reveal how deep-seated hydrous magmas interact with mantle-derived rocks to form amphibole, providing valuable insights into how continental crust differentiates and matures."

### Key Highlights
- **Deep Arc Crust Evolution:** Examines amphibole-rich ultramafic rocks from the Hida Metamorphic Complex to uncover how magmas differentiate beneath volcanic arcs.
- **Amphibole Reaction Textures:** Analyzes poikilitic amphibole enclosing corroded olivine grains, revealing that amphibole formed through reactions between hydrous melt and pre-existing mantle/cumulate rocks.
- **Continental Growth Mechanism:** Demonstrates how hydrous crystallization and magma differentiation deep within the crust control geochemical evolution in island arcs.
