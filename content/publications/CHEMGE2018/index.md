abstract: "Monazite is a common accessory mineral widely used to decipher past continental growth and sediment origins, but how its chemical composition changes in response to magma oxidation and differentiation has remained unclear. In this study, we investigated the rare earth elements, Th, U, and Nd isotopes of monazites from Japanese granitic rocks across both oxidized (magnetite-series) and reduced (ilmenite-series) types. Our results reveal distinct chemical fingerprints shaped by fractional crystallization and oxidation states, establishing robust geochemical criteria for tracking magma evolution and sediment provenance."

### Key Highlights
- **Geochemical Fingerprinting of Arc Granites:** Systematically examines trace elements (REEs, Th, U) and in situ Nd isotopes in monazites from magnetite- and ilmenite-series granites across the Japan arc.
- **Tracing Magma Differentiation & Redox:** Uncovers how crystallization of co-existing minerals (such as feldspar and apatite) and magma oxidation state control the chemical signatures of monazite.
- **Sediment Provenance Indicators:** Establishes diagnostic trace-element ratios to reliably distinguish monazite source rock types, enhancing its utility as a detrital provenance tracer.
