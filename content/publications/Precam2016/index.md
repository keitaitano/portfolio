abstract: "The African continent preserves vital clues about the ancient assembly of the Gondwana supercontinent hundreds of millions of years ago. In this study, we analyzed the ages and chemical 'fingerprints' of around 500 grains of the mineral monazite collected from sand along five major African rivers (Nile, Niger, Congo, Zambezi, and Orange). By dating these river sediments, we reconstructed the timing and metamorphism of the Pan-African mountain-building collision, demonstrating how river sands can record the grand tectonic history of entire continents."

### Key Highlights
- **River Sands as Continental Archives:** Analyzes ~500 detrital monazite grains across major African rivers (Nile, Niger, Congo, Zambezi, Orange) to map continent-scale mountain building and tectonic history.
- **Timing of Gondwana Assembly:** Pinpoints distinct metamorphic age peaks (around 630 to 500 Ma) corresponding to the Pan-African collision events that joined ancient landmasses into Gondwana.
- **Monazite vs. Zircon Clocks:** Shows that monazite ages are 20–40 million years younger than co-existing zircons in the same rivers, capturing the later-stage metamorphic and cooling events of continental collision.
