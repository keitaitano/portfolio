abstract: "Symplectic intergrowths preserved in mantle peridotites provide critical insight into early Earth geodynamics and fluid–rock interactions. Here, we report olivine-hosted spinel–diopside symplectites in Eoarchean dunites and decipher their petrogenetic origin. Textural and compositional evidence indicates that these unique microstructures formed through the dehydrogenation and oxidation of an OH-bearing precursor phase, recording ancient fluid activity and subsequent metasomatic/metamorphic reworking during the earliest stages of continental lithosphere evolution."

### Key Highlights
- **Discovery of Eoarchean Symplectic Textures:** Identifies and characterizes unique spinel–diopside symplectites hosted within olivine grains from ancient Eoarchean dunites.
- **Dehydrogenation-Oxidation Mechanism:** Demonstrates that the symplectite microstructures formed via dehydrogenation and oxidation of hydrous precursor minerals rather than simple breakdown of anhydrous phases.
